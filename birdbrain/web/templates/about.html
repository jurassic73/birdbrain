A camera watches the birdbath. A small computer at home notices movement, takes a quick burst of photos and picks the sharpest one. A small local model and then Claude decide who came to visit, and a few free nature sites fill in the rest: photos, songs, range maps and when each species is usually around.

Kept in place

Rocks get nudged by birds and refills, and a camera can shift. {% if recal.auto and recal.times %}Every day at {{ recal.labels | join(' and ') }}{% else %}When asked on the Settings page{% endif %} the watcher studies the bath for about a minute and takes the middle value of each pixel, so ripples, drifting leaves and birds fade out. It lines the bath's edges up with an earlier view: if the camera moved a few pixels, every zone moves with it (a big move is left for a hand calibration). Then it moves each rock spot back onto its rocks: by day the water reflects the sky and the rocks are dark, at night the water is black and the infrared light makes the rocks bright. Shapes far from a spot, like a sunken leaf, are ignored. Day and night each keep their own view to compare with, and any change can be undone on the Settings page. The zones can also be dragged into place by hand there: click the live photo, pick a shape and drag it, or add corners to the ground; later checks trust those: a rock spot keeps the size you gave it and only moves if the rocks leave it, and camera moves are measured from the view at that moment.

Describing the scene

On Claude's Notes you describe your yard in three boxes: what the camera sees, what to look for (the visitors you expect and their sizes) and what to ignore. Refine with Claude has Haiku tidy them into short lines that keep every fact, which saves tokens on every photo; you check the suggestion before saving it. Save and outline has Claude (Sonnet) look at a fresh frame and outline each thing you named, plus things likely to cause false alarms, like swaying branches. Round things (the bath, rocks, a birdhouse hole) are always ellipses, and the bath is one ring: its rim and the water inside. Claude's shapes are rough, so the Pi then lines them up with the photo using OpenCV: it slides the water's edge and the rim onto the strongest edges nearby, moves a rock onto the blob that stands out from the water (by day only; rocks barely show in night vision), and cuts the ground outline around the bowl. Anything it can't line up stays where Claude drew it, and the page marks what was "fitted to the photo". You get the last word: click a shape and drag its handles (a side to resize, the middle or the shape itself to move, a corner of the ground, add or remove corners, or switch a shape between an ellipse and points), Save keeps your changes (closing asks if you forgot), and Reset puts the automatic shape back. You accept the outline or try again with a better description; one costs a cent or two. The accepted objects and their notes go into every identification, distractions with the things to ignore. The zones above still come from the hand calibration.

{# ---------- 3. Detection rules ---------- #}

When movement counts as a visit

Rim and rocks

Each small-stream frame is compared with a slowly updating picture of the empty bath. A visit starts when at least {{ pct(cfg.MOTION_MIN_AREA) }}% of the watched area changes for {{ cfg.TRIGGER_FRAMES }} frames in a row (the dashed line). One flicker is not enough.

{% if cfg.WATER_WATCH and zones.water %}

Open water

Water sets off simple motion detectors constantly, so the open water has its own rule. It needs exactly one strong, compact blob between {{ pct(cfg.WATER_MIN_BLOB) }}% and {{ pct(cfg.WATER_MAX_BLOB) }}% of the water's area, staying near the same spot for {{ cfg.WATER_PERSIST_FRAMES }} frames in a row. Raindrop rings fade faster than that. It rests at night, when nothing flies in to land on the water. {% if presence_mode != 'off' %}Even then, the Pi's animal check looks first, so a drifting leaf never costs a Claude call.{% endif %}

{% endif %} {% if zones.ground %}

Ground around the bath

The lawn moves all day, so the ground has its own rule. It needs one animal-sized blob that stays in view for {{ cfg.GROUND_PERSIST_FRAMES }} frames in a row and moves at walking pace. By day a ground bird is big enough ({{ pct(cfg.GROUND_MIN_BLOB) }}% of the frame); at night it must be about cat-sized ({{ pct(cfg.GROUND_MIN_BLOB_NIGHT) }}%). Normally the photo goes straight to Claude, since the local model's labels don't include squirrels, raccoons or possums. A blowing leaf costs one cheap call and shows up under False triggers; after {{ cfg.PROBE_AFTER }} in a row the ground switches to local checks like the bath does (below).

{% endif %}

Cooldowns and rainy days

{% if cfg.STAY %}

One visit per stay. After a real visitor the visit stays open while it's still in view, anywhere on the bath or the ground: each frame is compared with the empty scene from just before it arrived, so a crow standing still still counts. No new visits start until it has been gone {{ cfg.STAY_GAP }} s, so a bird that bathes for five minutes is one visit, and its card says how long it stayed. Changes that sit still for {{ cfg.STAY_STILL }} s (leaves it flipped over) become part of the scene, a big change in the light ends the visit, and none runs past {{ (cfg.STAY_MAX / 60) | round | int }} minutes. It all runs on the Pi; Claude is asked once per visit.

{% endif %}

After a real visitor the camera also rests at least {{ cfg.COOLDOWN_VISIT }} s, so one splashing bird is one visit, not fifty. After a false alarm it waits {{ cfg.COOLDOWN_NONE }} s, and every false alarm in a row doubles the wait, up to {{ cfg.COOLDOWN_NONE_MAX }} s. A real visitor resets it.

When a visit starts, the Pi records {{ cfg.BURST_FRAMES }} full-size photos at {{ cfg.BURST_FPS }} per second and scores how sharp each one is{% if zones.crop %}, inside the crop box only, so busy ground around the bath can't win{% endif %}.{% if zones.ground %} A ground visit uses a wider crop around the ground zone instead.{% endif %} The sharpest goes to Claude; the next two are kept in case a second opinion is needed. {# ---------- 5. Local model ---------- #}

Checking on the Pi first

Before anything leaves the house, the Pi takes the first look with BioCLIP 2, an open-source model of living things. It answers two questions in order: is anything there, and what species is it? Claude is asked only when the Pi can't settle it. Each check runs in one of three modes, and a check earns its way from shadow to gate on its scorecard.

Anything there? A check trained on this yard{% if presence_mode != 'off' %} {{ badge(presence_mode, 'shadow' if presence_mode == 'shadow' else 'pi-only') }}{% endif %}

Most motion is leaves, ripples and light, and asking Claude about those is where nearly all the cost goes. So the Pi runs the same BioCLIP 2 model on the whole crop box and a small check, trained on this yard's own frames, decides whether anything is there. It learns from labels: every frame Claude answered, your corrections (they win), and the ones you mark "Looks right" or confirm on the False triggers page, which count extra. It retrains itself whenever a label changes, and calls a frame empty only if it looks emptier than every animal it has seen. {% if presence_mode == 'gate' %}It's in gate mode: a frame it judges empty skips Claude, day and night, and is kept as a false trigger marked {{ badge("pi only", "pi-only") }} for you to check. Correct any with an animal: that is exactly what it learns from. {% elif presence_mode == 'shadow' %}It's in shadow mode: it scores every frame, but Claude still sees everything.{% else %}It's switched off here.{% endif %} On the first 683 frames, tested on frames it hadn't trained on, it would have kept all 55 visitors and caught 616 of 628 empty frames. Night visitors (raccoons, possums) haven't come by yet, so it hasn't learned them. Scorecard on Stats.

A species guess of its own{% if classifier_mode != 'off' %} {{ badge('shadow', 'shadow') }}{% endif %}

{% if classifier_mode == 'off' %}Switched off here (CLASSIFIER=off). When on, a{% else %}After Claude answers, a{% endif %} second model on the Pi (BioCLIP 2 from the Imageomics Institute, open source, trained on millions of photos of living things including iNaturalist's; about 300 MB) guesses the species from a square around the bird Claude circled (or from the crop box). It knows every bird species in the eBird list, so its answers are limited to the birds {% if cfg.INAT_PLACE_ID %}reported to iNaturalist in this area{% else %}reported in the area, once an iNaturalist place is set{% endif %}. It works by matching, not by a fixed list of answers: the model turns the photo into a fingerprint of numbers, and every species already has a fingerprint made from a line of text ("a photo of Junco hyemalis with common name Dark-eyed Junco"), worked out once on a laptop so the Pi never has to. The guess is the species whose fingerprint is closest. The model is stored with 8-bit weights so it fits in the Pi's memory. It takes about 6 seconds on the Pi and costs nothing. It hasn't been tried on infrared night photos yet. For now it's in shadow mode: its top 3 guesses are shown under "How it was decided" on each visit and never change a label. {% if classifier.checked %}So far its first guess matched the final label on {{ classifier.top1 }} of {{ classifier.checked }} birds ({{ classifier.top3 }} within its top 3).{% endif %} The scorecard on Stats shows, species by species, when it could take over from Claude.

{# ---------- 6. Claude ---------- #}

Asking Claude, and asking again when unsure

  1. Haiku looks at the sharpest photo{{ ', cropped' if zones.crop }}
  2. Saw an animal? No False triggers, hidden from the gallery
  3. Sure of every animal? Yes Gallery and alerts. Also when fairly sure of a species already confirmed here {{ confirmed_min }}+ times (the Confirmed pill on Species)
  4. Sonnet looks at the 3 sharpest photos together, combining angles
  5. Still unsure? No Gallery and alerts
  6. Review queue: you have the final say

What Claude is told

  1. Instructions: the animal types, how to name species, how sure to be, that night photos are infrared
  2. Your yard from Claude's Notes: what the camera sees, what to look for, what to ignore{% if site.location %}, in {{ site.location }}{% elif not site.scene_notes %} (not filled in yet){% endif %}
  3. The objects Claude outlined in your scene: {{ site.objects | length if site.objects else 'none accepted yet' }}

{# ---------- 7. Learning ---------- #}

Learning from your corrections

  1. Claude answers"Dark-eyed Junco, bathing"
  2. You correct it"No animal: those are the rock tips after a refill"
  3. It becomes a lessonThe latest 15 corrections join every prompt
  4. Next photoClaude gets it right, starting with the very next visit

For Claude this is learning by example, not retraining: nothing about the models changes, they just read your recent corrections each time. The Pi's animal check does retrain: your corrections, and every frame you mark as right, become its training data. Claude also draws a dashed circle around what it identified (one per kind of animal), so you can see at a glance what it was looking at. Under "Also in the photo" you can add, remove or recount the other animals, and that becomes a lesson too. To point at something, circle it on the photo: each circle gets a number you can mention in your note ("1 is a rock, 2 is a leaf"), and the lesson tells Claude where each numbered circle sits in the frame. Your correction never erases Claude's original answer, and "first seen" dates use your label. The species boxes suggest names already seen here, then every bird on the eBird list as you type, so one species never ends up with two spellings. Not sure of the name? Type a clue ("red head, finch") and press Ask: Claude Sonnet looks at the photo and suggests the official name, with a button to use it (about a cent; nothing is saved until you press Save). Claude's Notes lists every lesson exactly as Claude reads it, next to your description and the whole prompt; switch off any that don't help and the next newest takes its place. {% if totals.corrected %}You've corrected or annotated {{ totals.corrected }} visit{{ 's' if totals.corrected != 1 }} so far.{% endif %}

{# ---------- 10. Under the hood ---------- #}

Under the hood

  • Camera Any camera with two RTSP streams; this project uses an inexpensive Wi-Fi camera with open-source firmware. Its picture and day/night settings can be changed from Settings > Camera, through the camera's own web API; every change is logged and can be undone.
  • Computer A Raspberry Pi on the home network runs the watcher, the local model, the database and this site.
  • Software Python with OpenCV for motion, ONNX Runtime for the local models, FastAPI and htmx for the pages, SQLite for the visits, Chart.js and Leaflet for charts and maps.
  • Privacy The site is only reachable at home, and photos and visit records stay on the Pi. What leaves the house: the cropped photo with your description and lessons from Claude's Notes (to Claude), the alert photo (to ntfy, if alerts are on), and species names and the iNaturalist place (to the info sources above). The Pi's species guesser runs at home; it only downloads its model once and the local bird list monthly. If a read-only public copy is set up, the Pi sends it the photos and visits every few minutes, but first a small local detector (YOLOX-tiny) checks every frame for people: a photo or live frame with a person in it never leaves the house, and one that hasn't been checked yet waits.
  • Storage Every real visit is kept. False alarms are kept up to a size limit (oldest first), except ones you've corrected, which are lessons.
